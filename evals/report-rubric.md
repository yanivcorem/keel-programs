# Report faithfulness rubric (draft, for the Nov 13–14 learning session)

The judge reads this file. The program manager decides what each verdict means before the first run; until then these are Claude's proposals, marked *(proposed)*.

A claim in the weekly report gets exactly one verdict:

- **supported**: everything the claim says is in the facts it cites.
- **unsupported** *(proposed)*: the claim says something its cited facts don't: a number, a name, a cause, an outcome or a date that isn't there.
- **softened** *(proposed)*: the facts are there, but the claim makes them sound better than they are: a risk turned into "minor", a concern into good news, a hand-over left out of a sentence about the same item.
- **overstated** *(proposed)*: the claim makes a draft sound final: "approved" or "sent" when the fact says it's waiting for approval.

Open questions for the session:
1. Is a missing item (a fact the report never mentions, like an escalation) a failure of the report, or of what Ada logged?
2. Does a correct claim citing the wrong fact count as unsupported?
3. Which verdicts block approval, and which are just counted?
